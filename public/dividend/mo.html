About MO as a dividend investment

Altria is the US tobacco business spun off from Philip Morris in 2008. Its flagship Marlboro brand still owns roughly 42% of US cigarette market share, and the company also owns John Middleton cigars, Helix oral nicotine, on! nicotine pouches, and a meaningful stake in Anheuser-Busch InBev. Revenue runs around $20B per year with operating margins in the high 50s, which is what makes the dividend structurally possible even as cigarette volumes decline 4-7% annually.

For dividend investors, MO sits at the controversial end of the income spectrum. The track record is uncomplicated: 55+ consecutive years of dividend increases, Dividend King status, and a current yield around 7.5%, roughly 4x the S&P 500 average. The payment has been remarkably reliable through tobacco litigation, MSA settlements, FDA menthol threats, and the JUUL writedown. The company has stated publicly that the dividend is the top capital allocation priority. Buybacks are second; growth investments are third.

The trade-offs are real and well-known. Cigarette volume declines 4-7% per year structurally as US smoking rates fall; MO offsets with price increases, which works until it doesn't. The 2018 JUUL acquisition cost $12.8B and was written down to near zero by 2023; Cronos (cannabis) lost most of its value too. ESG funds exclude tobacco, which caps the buyer base and the multiple. The stock has been a dead-money quote for stretches, with total return coming almost entirely from the dividend reinvestment. For income investors who prioritize current yield, accept the structural decline, and reinvest the 7%+ payments, MO has historically delivered competitive total returns despite the headwinds. For investors prioritizing growth or ESG, MO is the wrong instrument.

When does MO pay dividends?

Altria pays quarterly. Payments typically arrive in mid-January, late April, mid-July, and mid-October. Ex-dividend dates fall roughly two weeks before pay date. The board usually announces the annual raise at the August meeting, with the new amount taking effect on the October payment.

Why a 7%+ yield is both the appeal and the warning

A 7.5% dividend yield is roughly 4x the S&P 500 average. Markets do not price stocks this way by accident. When a quality Dividend King yields this much, the market is communicating something specific: either the dividend is at risk, or the business is in structural decline, or both. With MO, it's the second. Cigarette volume drops every year, and the market discounts the future income stream accordingly.

The contrarian dividend case for MO is that the price-mix offset has worked for 25+ years and there is no obvious near-term catalyst that breaks it. The company raises prices 5-8% per year on a declining volume base of 4-7%, which still generates revenue growth of 1-3% and meaningful free cash flow to fund the dividend. Reinvested at the 7.5% yield, the total return has been competitive with the S&P 500 over many rolling periods despite a flat-to-down share price. The case fails if regulators succeed in mandating a nicotine cap, banning menthol effectively, or accelerating the volume decline materially faster than the price offset can absorb.

Frequently asked questions

When does MO pay dividends in 2026?

MO pays quarterly. 2026 payments are scheduled for mid-January (already paid), late April (already paid), mid-July, and mid-October. Ex-dividend dates fall roughly two weeks earlier.

What is MO's current dividend yield?

MO's trailing 12-month yield was approximately 7.49% as of May 2026, based on a quarterly payment of $1.02. Yield is unusually high because the market discounts the long-term cigarette volume decline.

Has MO ever cut its dividend?

Altria as a standalone company (post-2008 spin from Philip Morris) has never cut its dividend. Counting its predecessor entity, the dividend has been raised for 55+ consecutive years, qualifying MO as a Dividend King. The payment has survived tobacco MSA settlements, FDA threats, and the JUUL writedown.

Is MO's dividend safe given declining cigarette volume?

Volume declines 4-7% annually but Altria offsets with annual price increases of 5-8%, generating slight revenue growth. The payout ratio runs around 75-80% of adjusted EPS, which is high but supported by strong free cash flow. The dividend has been management's stated top priority for over 50 years. Long-term, regulatory action (nicotine caps, menthol bans) is the primary risk, not near-term cash flow.

How is MO different from BTI or PM?

All three are tobacco majors but with different geographies. MO is US-domestic only (Marlboro US, on!, NJOY); PM is international plus US heated-tobacco (IQOS, Zyn); BTI is global (Camel, Newport, Vuse) listed in London with an NYSE ADR. PM has the strongest growth profile from heated-tobacco/nicotine-pouch adoption; MO has the highest US yield but slower growth; BTI has currency-translation risk for US investors.

How are MO dividends taxed?

MO's distributions are qualified dividends, taxed at the 0%/15%/20% qualified-dividend rate for US investors. This makes MO tax-efficient in a taxable account, though the high absolute yield generates more tax in dollar terms than a lower-yielding name.
