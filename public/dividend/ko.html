About KO as a dividend investment

Coca-Cola sells beverage concentrate to bottlers in over 200 countries. The business is one of the most cash-generative on earth: gross margins consistently above 60%, operating margins around 30%, and a brand portfolio (Coke, Sprite, Fanta, Costa Coffee, Topo Chico, BodyArmor) that gives the company pricing power most consumer-staples competitors can only envy.

For dividend investors, KO is held for three specific reasons. First, the 62 consecutive years of raises — every January since 1963, like clockwork — makes the next payment highly predictable in a way no growth stock can match. Second, the underlying business survives recessions: people kept buying Coke through 1973, 2001, 2008, and 2020. Third, the combination of ~3% yield and ~5% dividend growth produces a total dividend return that historically beats inflation by a comfortable margin.

The trade-off is growth. KO's revenue growth has averaged in the low single digits for over a decade, which means the stock itself rarely outperforms the S&P 500 in bull markets. Income investors who own KO aren't expecting it to be a top performer in a given year — they're expecting it to keep paying and keep raising through every economic cycle, which it has done for six decades.

When does KO pay dividends?

KO pays quarterly. Payments typically arrive in April, July, October, and January, with ex-dividend dates roughly two weeks earlier. The board announces each declaration on its regular schedule, and Coca-Cola has never delayed or paused a payment in its modern history.

What "Consumer Staples dividends" means for income investors

Consumer Staples is the GICS sector covering companies that sell things people buy regardless of the economy — beverages (KO, PEP), household products (PG, CL), tobacco (MO, PM), and packaged food (GIS, K). The sector's defining characteristic for dividend investors is cash flow stability: revenue holds up in recessions because demand for soda, soap, and cigarettes doesn't collapse the way demand for cars, vacations, or homes does.

This stability is why Consumer Staples is overrepresented among Dividend Kings and Aristocrats. Of the ~50 Dividend Kings (50+ consecutive years of raises), more than a quarter are staples companies. KO is one of the original anchors of the category.

Frequently asked questions

When does KO pay dividends in 2026?

KO pays quarterly. 2026 payments are scheduled around April 1, July 1, October 1, and the following January. Ex-dividend dates fall roughly two weeks earlier, in mid-March, mid-June, mid-September, and late November.

What is KO's current dividend yield?

KO's trailing 12-month yield was approximately 2.96% as of April 2026, based on a $0.505 quarterly payment. Yield moves inversely with share price.

Is KO a Dividend King?

Yes. Coca-Cola has raised its dividend for 62 consecutive years (since 1963), well past the 50-year threshold that defines a Dividend King. KO is one of the most established Kings in the consumer-staples sector.

How much has KO's dividend grown over time?

KO's 5-year dividend CAGR is approximately 4.8%. The annual raise is announced each February for the following January payment — and has happened every year since 1963.

Is KO a good dividend stock for income investors?

KO is held by income-focused investors for its 62-year track record, recession-resistant cash flows, and the combination of ~3% yield with ~5% dividend growth. The trade-off is slower price appreciation than growth stocks. This is not investment advice; consult a licensed advisor for your situation.

KO vs PEP — which is better for dividend investors?

Both are Dividend Kings in consumer staples. KO typically yields slightly higher (~3.0% vs ~2.8%) but grows more slowly (~4.8% vs PEP's ~7%). KO has 62 consecutive years of raises; PEP has 52. Many income portfolios hold both for sector diversification.
